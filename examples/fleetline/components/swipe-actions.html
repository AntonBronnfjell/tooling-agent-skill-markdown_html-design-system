Swipe actions

Usage

Use swipe actions on the mechanics' tablet and phone lists, where a row's two or three most frequent actions save a trip into the work order: mark it done, reassign it, delete a duplicate.

  • Trailing swipe (toward the inline start) reveals up to three actions; the edge action is the one a full swipe runs.
  • Leading swipe reveals one positive action (Mark done).
  • Always put the same actions in the row's More actions menu. A swipe is a shortcut, never the only way (WCAG 2.5.7), and desktop dispatchers never swipe.
  • Don't hide an action people need to discover (put it in the menu or on the detail page), or use a full swipe for something that can't be undone without an Undo toast.

Content: one or two words per action, a verb ("Reassign", "Delete"). The destructive action says what it does in the menu ("Delete work order").

Anatomy

  1. Row — <li class="swipe-row"> with --_lead and --_trail action counts
  2. Track — .swipe-row__track, the horizontal scroll-snap container
  3. Leading actions — .swipe-row__actions--leading, inert until revealed
  4. Content — .swipe-row__content: link (stretched over the row), secondary text and the More actions menu button
  5. Trailing actions — .swipe-row__actions--trailing, edge action last; its color fills the full-swipe zone

Do and don't

Accessibility

Keyboard interaction
KeyBehavior
TabRow link, then the More actions button. Hidden swipe actions are inert and skipped
Enter / Space on More actionsOpens the menu with the same actions (APG menu button)
  • Pointer gestures and dragging (WCAG 2.5.1, 2.5.7): every swipe action is also a menu item reachable with one tap or click.
  • Pointer cancellation (2.5.2): nothing runs while swiping; a full swipe runs only when the row settles past the threshold, and moving back cancels it.
  • Names: action buttons have visible text; the menu button's name includes the row ("More actions for WO-4182 Brake inspection").
  • Deleting a row moves focus to the next row's link and announces "Work order deleted" with an Undo button in a toast.
  • Reduced motion: rows close without smooth scrolling. RTL: leading and trailing flip with the writing direction.
  • Forced colors: action buttons keep a ButtonText border; the focus ring uses Highlight.
